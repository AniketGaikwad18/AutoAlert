About the Project

The idea for AutoAlert came from a real-life experience. One day, while I was travelling to college, I saw an ambulance stuck in heavy traffic. There was barely any space for it to move, and I could see that the patient inside was in a critical condition. I felt helpless watching the situation and started thinking about how technology could help emergency vehicles and accident victims receive help faster.

As an engineering student, I wanted to turn that frustration into a solution. I began thinking: what if existing CCTV cameras could detect an accident themselves and immediately call for help?

That thought led to AutoAlert—a system that adds an AI Box to existing CCTV cameras. The system analyzes the camera feed, detects events such as vehicle crashes or a person lying motionless, verifies the incident, and can automatically alert emergency services with the exact location and incident footage.

What I Learned

Building this idea helped me understand that solving a real-world problem is not only about creating an AI model. It is also about thinking about deployment, cost, existing infrastructure, false alarms, emergency communication, and scalability. I learned how AI can be combined with hardware, cameras, GPS, communication systems, and cloud services to create a practical smart-city solution.

How I Built the Concept

Instead of replacing the millions of CCTV cameras already installed, AutoAlert uses a small, weatherproof AI Box attached beside an existing camera. The proposed box contains a mini computer, SIM module, GPS, microphone, storage, and battery backup.

The system follows a simple flow:

CCTV → AI Detection → Incident Verification → GPS Location → Emergency Alert → Response

Challenges

The biggest challenge was designing a system that could be fast, affordable, and reliable at the same time. A false alarm could waste emergency resources, while a missed accident could cost a life. We therefore designed an incident-verification step before sending an alert. Another challenge was making the solution scalable without replacing existing CCTV infrastructure.

AutoAlert started with one ambulance stuck in traffic—and became my attempt to build a city where technology can recognize an emergency and ask for help before someone else has to.
